# Provena backend: EUDR gap analysis

Research date: **12 October 2026**. Repository baseline: `5607cf5802f53426df0dec2f754230f8c33eb492`.

This is a research and discovery report, not a compliance determination or an approved implementation specification. It assesses the supplied backend, not a deployed service, customer procedures, or the separately maintained frontend. Only this document is changed.

## 1. Method, evidence and priority

`AGENTS.md` was read first. Repository findings come from inspected entities, DTOs, validators, services, endpoint registration, EF mappings, migration declarations and test source. Documentation helped locate and interpret project decisions; it was not used as proof of implementation. In particular, [ADR 0002](adr/0002-supplier-producer-production-plot.md) is an accepted **target direction**, not evidence that Producer or ProductionPlot has been implemented.

The report separates:

- **Regulatory requirements:** the binding provisions identified in each matrix row, read with their amendments and application dates.
- **Repository observations:** behavior directly visible in source. Named tests are inspected expectations, not passing runtime evidence.
- **Recommendations:** proposed product/data-model responses. The legislation does not prescribe Provena's entity names, database tables, UI, or automation.
- **Unknowns:** facts or interpretations not established by this review. “Not found / not evidenced in the repository” does not exclude an external manual process, deployment-specific integration, or a capability outside this checkout.

Searches covered application/test C# and project files for risk, DDS, EORI, Information System/TRACES, producer, plot, batch, retention, deforestation and legality concepts. Negative findings were cross-checked against the domain model, `AppDbContext`, service registration and endpoint inventory. Uploaded files and free-text fields are not treated as structured capabilities or verified evidence of compliance.

**P0** = required for a credible operator pilot; **P1** = important for production readiness; **P2** = later enhancement. These are product priorities, not statutory rankings. The working pilot assumption is an operator handling a narrowly selected set of relevant products, with a named human responsible for due diligence and manual use of the EU Information System. Full due diligence is the conservative product default unless Article 13 eligibility is documented. This is a recommendation, not a legal conclusion about any customer. Conditional P0 items become blockers if the pilot includes the relevant role, commodity or transaction.

An external controlled process may address a P0 gap, but its owner, retained evidence, case linkage and release control must be explicit. Calling a missing capability “manual” without establishing those controls does not close the gap.

Article 38(2) sets **30 December 2026** for the principal obligations listed there. Article 38(3) provides **30 June 2027** for qualifying natural-person/micro/small operators established as such by 31 December 2024, except products covered by the EUTR Annex. Do not apply the later date automatically to every small business or downstream actor. Articles 1(2) and 37(2)–(3) separately address pre-29 June 2023 production and transitional timber; applicability requires transaction and production history. On the research date, preparation for these obligations is not the same as a finding that a customer has already breached them. [Articles 1(2), 37 and 38][eudr]

The September scope amendment matters now: it removes specified cattle hides/leather entries; adjusts other product descriptions/exclusions; and adds, with deferred application, frozen cattle tongues, coffee extracts and specified palm derivatives. These are adopted changes, not the earlier draft scope proposal. A production scope catalogue must encode entry-specific dates and conditions. [Regulation 2026/2102, Annex points (1)–(6), particularly (6)(b), (c), (e), (g)][scope-2026]

### Substantive distinctions behind the matrix

**Full risk assessment:** Article 10(2)(a)–(n) addresses country classification; forests; indigenous peoples, consultation and land claims; deforestation/degradation prevalence; reliability and validity of evidence; corruption, falsification, enforcement, human-rights, conflict and sanctions concerns; supply-chain complexity; circumvention/mixing; Commission expert-group findings; substantiated concerns and past non-compliance; other risk indicators; and complementary certification/verification information. Article 10(1), (4) requires a reasoned risk determination and at least annual review. Article 11 requires adequate mitigation where risk is not negligible, records of decisions, and applicable governance controls. A numeric country score or an accepted document does not by itself demonstrate these steps. [Articles 10–11][eudr]

**Information dossier versus statement:** Article 9's evidence dossier is broader than the Annex II DDS fields. Production dates, supplier/buyer contacts, legality evidence and assessment records must not be assumed to be identical to the external submission payload. Conversely, organization address/EORI where applicable, statement product description/quantity, all required locations, declaration and signature information must be available for DDS preparation. The report does not recommend sending the whole operational ZIP to the EU system. [Article 9; Annex II points 1–3, 5–6][eudr]

**Customs boundary:** Article 26(4) requires the relevant reference/identifier before release for free circulation or export, but expressly excludes exports by a downstream operator from that paragraph. Article 28(1)'s EU customs interface deadline is 1 December 2029; it is not a requirement that Provena implement that interface now. A customs reference, an Information System reference and a legal assessment remain different records. [Articles 26(4), 28(1)][eudr]

**Retention starts differ:** operator DDS records run five years from statement submission (Article 4(3), subject to Article 4a); Article 9 information runs five years from placing/export; downstream Article 5(3) information is retained at least five years from placing/making available/export; due-diligence documentation is retained at least five years under Article 12(5), with system-update records addressed by Article 12(2). Case creation/completion is not a universal substitute for those events. The EU system's own personal-data storage rules are not Provena's retention policy. [EUDR Articles 4(3), 4a(1), 5(4), 9(1), 12(2), (5)][eudr]; [2024/3084 Article 12(5), as amended][is-amend]

## 3. What the backend supports today

The inspected implementation supports a tenant-scoped shipment evidence workflow:

- **Catalogues and shipments:** `CatalogService` manages Supplier and Product; `ShipmentService.Save` creates one shipment referencing one supplier and one product, one case, and six required checklist items. Lists, case filters, dashboard counts and CSV imports are present. [CatalogService][catalog-service], [ShipmentService][shipment-service], [ImportService][import-service], [CaseService][case-service]
- **Supplier evidence collection:** a hashed capability token grants access to one request/case; rotation, revocation, expiry and post-lock checks are implemented. Details contain one current country, period, quantity and notes. Public completion records submission, not reviewer acceptance. [SupplierRequestService.Resolve, LockPublic, SaveDetails, Complete][request-service]
- **Files and geometry:** multiple documents per case, categories, upload metadata, downloads and deletion; one current case-level GeoSubmission. Polygon/MultiPolygon topology and coordinate bounds are checked. This is structural validation. [CaseFileService][file-service], [GeoJsonValidationService][geo-service]
- **Internal review:** accept/reject submitted requirements or mark requirements `NotRequired`; derive operational case status; complete only after recalculation yields `ReadyForExport`. `CompleteInput.ExternalDdsReference` is optional and length-validated. [CaseService.Review/Complete][case-service], [EudrCaseStatusService.Calculate][status-service], [CompleteValidator][validators]
- **Export and archival:** ZIP with manifest, CSV summary, documents and original geometry; export is available without a readiness gate and explicitly disclaims a legal conclusion. Shipment archival retains evidence and revokes requests. [ExportService.Export][export-service], [ShipmentService.Archive][shipment-service]
- **Persistence and accountability foundation:** tenant filters, composite tenant foreign keys, case locks, workflow audit events and an audit modification/deletion guard in `SaveChangesAsync(CancellationToken)`. These are useful controls, not a universal database immutability or retention guarantee. [AppDbContext][db-context], [CaseService.Lock][case-service], [AuditService.Add][audit-service]

The inspected tests include `WorkflowTests.Full_http_flow_and_organization_isolation` and `Token_rotation_revocation_and_expiry_are_enforced`; `DomainTests` covers status priority, CN format, geometry and file validation; `EndpointInventoryTests` covers routes and access metadata. No tests were executed for this documentation-only task. [WorkflowTests][workflow-tests], [DomainTests][unit-tests], [EndpointInventoryTests][endpoint-tests]

## 5. Interpretation, model implications and verification limits

### Concrete model implications to take into later feature planning

These are recommendations, not approved schema changes:

1. **Applicability records:** legal entity/establishment and activity-specific role, size eligibility evidence, legal-rule version, classification date, relevant market event and decision rationale. Separate regulatory role from account permissions.
2. **Production provenance:** separate counterparty, producer and plot/establishment identities; revisioned geometry; production events and dates; product/lot-to-source links. Define cardinalities and ownership rather than backfilling identities from arbitrary GeoJSON properties.
3. **Quantified lineage:** identifiable lots, shipment lines where needed, source allocations and onward recipients; preserve units, conversion provenance, composition and processing/split/merge history for supported flows. Quantity allocation is a proposed control, not a mandated EUDR SQL schema.
4. **Due-diligence record:** evidence-to-requirement links, country-classification version, path eligibility, criteria/rationale, mitigation actions, residual-risk decision, accountable sign-off and future review/incident links.
5. **External statement records:** own versus incoming DDS, simplified declarations, receipts, external status and coverage relations; reference numbers distinct from access-enabling verification numbers; retain the exact facts submitted.
6. **Historical preservation:** protected evidence versions and reviewed catalogue snapshots; retention event dates, retrieval and recovery controls. The current one-case/one-shipment organization may remain an operational view without constraining legal statement coverage.

A controlled single-product, single-lot pilot may defer broader line/processing functionality. It still needs complete source coverage and an explicit restriction against unsupported flows. A pilot excluding wood/cattle or Article 4a must say so; the existing commodity enum is not evidence that these paths are ready.

## 6. Executive summary

**Provena currently provides a useful operational evidence-collection and review foundation. The inspected repository does not evidence a complete operator due-diligence system.** Its strongest implemented capabilities are tenant-scoped catalogues and shipments, supplier capability links, evidence intake, structural geometry checks, checklist review, audit events and operational ZIP export. [Sections 3–4](#3-what-the-backend-supports-today)

The central product gap is not merely a missing DDS connector. It is the absence of an evidenced chain from **applicability → identified production sources and quantities → sufficient evidence → reasoned due-diligence decision → external statement coverage → retained, reproducible records**. All six checklist items being accepted or waived cannot establish that chain. A manual EU submission boundary can remain appropriate for a pilot if the underlying record and accountable process are complete.

The amended framework requires separate treatment of operators, downstream operators/traders and qualifying micro/small primary operators. Annex I also changed in September 2026. Designing against original 2023 roles, old deadlines or a static list of seven commodity names would misstate the current baseline. [Sections 2 and 4](#2-regulatory-baseline-and-authority)

This report makes no finding about a customer's legal compliance. Recommendations require owner approval and, for applicability, evidence sufficiency and legal interpretation, competent legal/domain input.

## 7. Top 5 P0 gaps

1. **Applicability and product scope are not established:** role, activity, timing, Annex I entry and applicable exception need an evidenced determination before checklist selection. **G01–G03**, with conditional **G05**.
2. **Article 9 production and transaction provenance is incomplete:** counterparty addresses/recipients, producer/plot identity, complete geolocation/date coverage, quantities and lot-to-source lineage. **G06–G16**, with commodity-specific conditions.
3. **Evidence is not connected to a reasoned risk decision:** legality/deforestation findings, Article 13 justification or full Article 10 assessment, mitigation and authorised release controls are not evidenced. **G17–G20, G22**.
4. **DDS coverage and external release evidence are not established:** an optional text reference and ZIP do not establish an Annex II statement, submission receipt, covered products/quantities, or onward reference communication. **G09, G21**, with a manual boundary permitted as a recommendation.
5. **Historical evidence and required retention are not secured by the current model:** overwritten submissions/geometry, deletable documents and live catalogue reads limit reconstruction of the reviewed/submitted record. **G27–G28**, with incident follow-up under **G23**.

## 8. Suggested implementation order

1. **Approve a pilot envelope and operating procedure.** Decide actor, activity, countries, products, commodities, transaction complexity, responsible reviewer and manual EU submission owner. Resolve G01–G03 and conditional exclusions before feature commitments.
2. **Design provenance and retention together.** Agree producer/plot/establishment identity, legal entity/contact data, production events, lots/allocations, historical snapshots and retention. Avoid collecting new evidence into a model that discards its history. Address G06–G16 and G27–G28; future schema work must follow the existing [database migration rules](DATABASE.md).
3. **Build the evidence and due-diligence decision record.** Add country-classification versioning, Article 13 qualification, full-risk criteria where applicable, legality/deforestation findings, mitigation and accountable sign-off. Cover G17–G20/G22 and a manual incident process under G23.
4. **Close the manual statement and release loop.** Produce reviewed Annex II data, retain external receipts/coverage, record downstream recipients/reference handoff, and demonstrate historical retrieval for representative pilot cases. Cover G09/G21. Obtain external process evidence before calling the operator pilot credible.
5. **Prepare production operations and remaining role/commodity paths.** Governance review/reporting, incident automation, recovery/retention automation, downstream and Article 4a paths, and broader lot/line transformations as agreed. P1 does not defer a statutory duty if that flow is already supported.
6. **Automate only after those records are stable.** Optional EU API integration, conformance tests, lifecycle/outage reconciliation, external analytical providers and voluntary grouping. Validate actual EU environment behavior rather than relying solely on documentation.

Each future implementation is a separate approved repository change with its own contracts, migrations where needed, tests and documentation. This analysis authorizes none of those changes.

## 9. Questions requiring owner/legal clarification

1. **Pilot responsibility:** Which legal entity and activities are in scope: EU importer, EU producer, exporter, downstream processor, trader, or authorised representative? Can one tenant represent multiple legal entities/roles? Who approves the assessment?
2. **Product and temporal envelope:** Which exact Annex I entries, commodities, countries and first transaction dates are intended? Are mixed products, cattle, wood, legacy timber or newly added December 2027 products included? Who signs off tariff classifications and exceptions?
3. **Size/regime eligibility:** Is the Article 4a primary-operator route required? Who verifies establishment/size and relevant-activity calculations? Is Article 13 simplification a pilot requirement or may the pilot use full due diligence throughout?
4. **Provenance identity:** What supplier-to-producer and producer-to-plot relationships are allowed? How are cooperatives, shared plots, changing tenure, duplicate identities and cross-tenant sharing handled? Who may correct a source record and on what evidence?
5. **Transaction coverage:** Must a case represent one lot, one shipment, a period of supply or another unit? Are splits, merges and processing supported? Who defines quantity reconciliation/conversion rules and DDS-to-product coverage?
6. **Evidence standard:** Which country/commodity-specific evidence is acceptable for land-use rights, legality, deforestation and forest degradation? Who assesses authenticity, contradictions, FPIC and third-party rights? Which external specialists or data providers are relied upon?
7. **Decision authority:** Who may approve due diligence, declare an item inapplicable or authorise release? Must separation of duties be enforced? What grounds justify waivers, and what information is never waivable within the selected legal path?
8. **External submission boundary:** Does the operator or a mandated representative submit manually? What receipt/status and signature evidence must return to Provena? Is an API connector actually required, and who owns operator credentials and verification-number access?
9. **Retention and incident handling:** Which market/export/submission events start each retention clock? What holds, storage, privacy, recovery and disposal rules apply? How are new concerns after completion investigated and communicated without altering the historical decision?
10. **Claims and acceptance:** Is the pilot explicitly an evidence-workflow pilot or intended to support the operator's complete due-diligence process? What external controls and independently reviewed sample cases are required before making that claim?
